How to Study Efficiently

1. Put everything in one place

At the start of each semester, add all your classes to the planner, so nothing is kept in your head or on scraps of paper.

2. Add assessments as soon as you get them

Enter the title and due date the day you get the brief. The planner lists them in date order, so the next deadline is always at the top.

3. Break big tasks into steps

Add checklist steps to each assessment, like plan, research, draft and hand in. Tick each one off as you finish it, and the progress bar fills up by itself.

4. Check the calendar every week

Look for weeks where several deadlines are close together, and start those assessments early.

5. Study in short, focused sessions

Work for about 25 minutes with your phone away, then take a 5-minute break. Short sessions are easier to start and help you concentrate.

6. Ask for help early

If you're stuck or falling behind, use the support services below before the deadline, not after.

Help with funding, travel, health and course guidance. A good first stop if you're not sure who to ask.

Extra support with your learning, such as exam arrangements or help if you have a disability or learning difference.

Student Counselling

Free, confidential sessions to talk through stress, worries or anything personal, with someone outside your day-to-day life.

Student LGBT+

Support, advice and community for LGBT+ students, in a safe and welcoming space.

Student Community

Clubs, events and activities to meet people and get more out of college life.

Career

Advice on jobs, CVs, applications and what to do after your course, whatever stage you're at.

Library

Books, study spaces and study-skills guides on every campus. A good place to start any assessment.

Accommodation

Help finding somewhere to live while you study, and what to sort out after you accept your place.

External Support

Organisations outside the college that can help with things like money, housing and mental health.
