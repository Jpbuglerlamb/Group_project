How to Study Efficiently

  1. Put everything in one place. At the start of each semester, add all your classes to the planner, so nothing is kept in your head or on scraps of paper.
  2. Add assessments as soon as you get them. Enter the title and due date the day you get the brief. The planner lists them in date order, so the next deadline is always at the top.
  3. Break big tasks into steps. Use the progress slider as a checklist: for example 25% when you've planned, 50% when you've researched, 75% when you've drafted and 100% when it's handed in.
  4. Check the calendar every week. Look for weeks where several deadlines are close together, and start those assessments early.
  5. Study in short, focused sessions. Work for about 25 minutes with your phone away, then take a 5-minute break. Short sessions are easier to start and help you concentrate.
  6. Ask for help early. If you're stuck or falling behind, use the support services below before the deadline, not after.

Student Counselling

Counselling can help you to make sense of your distress and emotional difficulties, enabling you to find meaningful ways to support yourself through your unique challenges. Although many personal issues can be shared with friends or family, it can be helpful to speak to someone who is neutral and outside of your day-to-day experiences. Students throughout each campus can access the counselling service, benefiting from a confidential and safe space to talk regularly through emotional concerns, finding solutions to personal obstacles and developing their self-care. For many of us, it isn’t easy to understand our experiences or put them into words.
